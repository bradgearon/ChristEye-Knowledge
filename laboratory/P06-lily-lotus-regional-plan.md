# Lily, Lotus and the waters

## The pair

Lily and Lotus are essential counterparts in Anaiero. The designer gives both the same title, **the high one**, and distinguishes how each is high.

| Counterpart | Designer’s words | Place in the design |
|---|---|---|
| Lotus | “The high one by suffering” | Its petals are the part that **Hears**. Lotus coordinates what has been received. |
| Lily | “The high one by giving” | Its biological study asks what one participant gives that makes receipt, passage or placement possible for another. |

The image is to “separate the waters from the waters” (Genesis 1:6–7). *Above* and *below* are prepositions. They locate something relative to the water. *By* names how each is high. The petals are the parts with **offset origins** in the two counterparts. The design records which petal belongs to which counterpart and the origin from which it extends. The physical reference for an offset, its magnitude and any cellular identity for a petal are open questions. “Hears” is the designer’s name for reception at the Lotus petals.

## First digital check

Use two distinguishable inputs and a named water boundary. In a synthetic example, record the counterpart, petal, petal origin, reference for any offset, source of each input, Lotus petal that received it, and the subsequent decision. A petal’s origin and the source of a signal arriving there are separate facts. If a spatial reference becomes available, record its frame and units. Until then, named origins suffice to test reception and provenance.

Present each input alone, then both together. Check the **Hears** record before reading the coordination decision. When both inputs arrive, both source–recipient associations should still be recoverable. Use a second event to test precedence, continued coexistence, or deferral. Swap the inputs between receivers and check that their source labels follow them. An input diverted upstream and an input received but deferred should leave distinguishable records. This is a proposed digital test for the existing Lotus adviser.

## P06 human regional comparisons

| Study and scope | Participants and evidence | What Anaiero would measure |
|---|---|---|
| Spinal floor plate, first model of giving guidance | Human floor-plate organoids promoted ventral patterning, guidance of commissural axons and bilateral connectivity in spinal assembloids. [Onesto et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq7934) | Identify the organizer, source neurons, responding axons and opposite receiving region. With matched geometry and support, compare the fraction of traced commissural axons that cross with and without the organizer. Record an evoked response at the receiver as a separate outcome. |
| Midline zipper glia, forebrain comparison for giving access | These glia remodel the interhemispheric fissure to create a crossing site for callosal axons. Evidence combines human anatomical observations with causal experiments in mice. [Gobius et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2016.09.033) | Measure remodeling at the boundary before scoring axons reaching or crossing it. This distinguishes a route becoming available from an axon taking that route. |
| Cajal–Retzius (CR) cells, optional cortical comparison for origins and placement | Human medial pallium organoids yield TP73/RELN-positive CR cells. Mouse fate mapping found several sites of origin and subsequent tangential migration. Mouse experiments also distinguish the contribution of CR-derived Reelin to neuronal positioning. [Amin et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2024.10.016) · [Bielle et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1511) · [Reelin study, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2120079119) | Track CR birthplace, path and final position, then independently measure the placement of neighboring neurons. A cortical comparison can test what CR cells contribute when other conditions match. Glutamatergic output has its own direct evidence in mouse hippocampal slices and would need its own readout here. [Quattrocolo & Maccaferri, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1407-14.2014) |
