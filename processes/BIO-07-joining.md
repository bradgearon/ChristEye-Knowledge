# BIO-07 · Joining neural networks, axon routes and the electrical Word interface

**Purpose:** discriminate between digital messaging, measured electronic neural bridging, extracellular shared context and true axonal/synaptic joining.

## Connection layers

| Layer | Actual mechanism | Evidence for qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Digital WindBus | authenticated/versioned payload, ACL, receipt | delivery ordering, source authority and replay control |
| Host neural interface | OEM supported electrode/optical channels, readout mapping | artifacts, stimulation isolation, delivered-event clocks and sensor validity |
| Electronic C-to-B teaching | decoded source observation → fixed transform → physical input of separate recipient | appropriate controls and recipient-only tests |
| Shared environmental coupling | culture exchange or common fields | separate source/recipient viability, fluid/media flow and common context |
| Axonal joint / synapse | anatomical projections and synaptic influence | registered axon trajectories, presynaptic/postsyanptic organization, functional influence, common-drive controls |

The initial system **must** preserve independently supported C, O, S and B physical modules; do not assume cell co-culture or common feed is electrically equivalent. A biological fusion/axon-joining study is a later qualified lab campaign, never a software command.

## Electrical and temporal codebook

A proposed teaching "Word" is the structured cue **as actually delivered**, including encoding/cue identity, electrode/region map, timing, bounds, physical receipt and artifact mask. The electronic host cannot decide the relevant biological meaning from the waveform alone. Inputs C0/C1, AB/BA and GO are digital reference-task symbols until a preparation-specific transfer function is validated. See [BIO-06](BIO-06-teaching.md).

**Bridge record**: link_id, source_preparation, target_preparation, source_observation, transform_version, codebook_version, intended_message_content, actual delivered event, clock anchor/uncertainty, shared support status, route status, protection permit, joined experiment ID, assay readout, reviewer.

## Connection verification

1. Measure C baseline and B baseline separately; seal B's frozen reader.
2. Demonstrate a route under *instrument-only* synthetic conditions; confirm no hidden data or training labels enter the recipient.
3. Observe source input and recipient response under authorized read-only conditions; record direct electrical vs shared fluid/environment confounds.
4. Compare same timing/energy with different informational content and mismatched maps *only as approved by instrument scientists*. A matched replay may still convey answers.
5. Distinguish *assisted joined use* from *recipient acquired knowledge*. The latter requires B alone after removal of teaching routes, caches and shared adaptive state.
6. Check protected old tasks and independent support continuity for both modules before any expansion.

Published 2025 and 2026 assembloid/connectoid experiments [B02](../research/evidence/anaiero-biology-2026-11.md), [B03](../research/evidence/anaiero-biology-2026-11.md) show examples of recorded interregional connectivity but do not provide a universal method for Anaiero.

## What connects at the molecular boundary?

Axons, receptor/ligand interaction sites, synapses and glial/ECM context. Candidate presynaptic SYN1/SYP/RIM/Munc13, postsynaptic DLG4/SHANK/HOMER, receptor identities and tracer/readout markers are *assay questions*, not an ingredient list. Physical axons are not WinBus packets and adding synaptic proteins is not equivalent to growing a connection.

## Failure and food/peptide-change card

Route success cannot be credited to an experimental chemical addition without isolating molecular effects from general firing/support. Any candidate modification requires qualified target expression, source identity, route-specific hypothesis, approved lab comparator, sensor and Root preservation, and a signed stop plan.
