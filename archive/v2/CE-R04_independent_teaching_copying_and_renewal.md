# 1 Purpose and boundary

Tests whether a trained living module improves acquisition in another and whether that improvement remains when the teacher and shared digital state are removed. It does not equate same lineage, matching responses or source-cell cloning with copied memory.

# 2 Inputs

- Teacher A baseline and frozen interface, recipient B baseline

- Bridge events with delivered content and timing recorded

- Software-teacher, shuffled, untrained and isolated control plans

# 3 Outputs / Winds

- B-only retained-performance report and effect estimate

- Description of assisted access versus independent acquisition

- Copying limitation statement and future experiment proposals

# 4 Required behavior

- Report labels and task information transmitted by A in full.

- Require recipient-only tests after bridge removal and cache clearing.

- Compare identical information from conventional software teaching.

- Preserve source, learned history and newly acquired state as different things.

# 5 Processing and activation

> **1.** Demonstrate teacher competency independently.
>
> **2.** Train recipient under matched teaching scenarios.
>
> **3.** Disconnect teacher and clear contingent shared state.
>
> **4.** Assess recipient after a declared delay on fresh tasks.
>
> **5.** Report effect versus the strongest matched control.

# 6 Test and release gate

- A bridge-on score cannot count as independent B learning.

- No copy claim without selective retained relation after A removal.

- A replacement module gets a new biological identity and acceptance record.

# 7 Failure and protection

- Hidden teacher cues through shared normalization or cached outputs.

- Functional replacement confused with restoration of original memory.

# 8 Open research and engineering questions

- Does a curriculum-derived signal outperform identical labels from a digital teacher?
